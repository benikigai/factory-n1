The problem

A factory floor gets drawn once.

Then demand shifts. Products change. Peak days hit. The floor stays the same, because redesigning it is slow, expensive and risky.

AI agents can propose the redesign. But agents grade their own homework. They report wins they didn't earn. One of ours invented a simulator run that never happened.

So we flipped it. The agents draw. Code decides.

Version N+1

Every change is a revision. Nemotron designers research, propose a new floor and predict its score. A frozen simulator runs 100 packages through it. The revision ships only if it beats the current floor by at least 1% on 9 days the designers never saw, and no goal check gets worse.

Rev 0 failed the goal. Rev 7 cut the score by 66% and still broke fragile packages. Rev 11 moved one pack station 3 cells inland and met the goal on all 15 development days.

What's next

Today the nodes are stations. Receive, sort, pack, ship.

Next they are humanoid robots, vision models and world models. Each one drops in as a node the simulator can score. The agents design around it. The gate stays the same.

The loop works for anything a simulator can measure. A fulfillment floor is the first one.

The floor is never finished. It ships its next revision.
